EPS and the degree of financial leverage are single-period arithmetic on the declared EBIT, interest, tax and share count; no dilution, buyback or preference dividend is modelled. The interest tax shield is interest times the tax rate and assumes the interest is deductible and the firm is profitable enough to use it. The Altman Z-score is the classic five-term formula computed from your declared figures; no zone cut-off is applied, because the thresholds are sample-specific and not verified here. The indifference EBIT is where the two declared plans happen to report the same EPS. It is not a recommendation to borrow or to issue shares, and it ignores risk, covenants and issue costs.

Assumptions made
EBIT, interest, tax and the share count are one period's declared figures.
Interest is tax-deductible and the firm has taxable profit.
The Altman inputs are all from the same date when used.
The alternative plan's interest and share count are the only things that change.
Limitations
No choice between the plans: the indifference point is arithmetic, not a recommendation.
No dilution, buyback or preference dividend.
The Altman Z-score is a model, not a probability of default; no zone cut-off is applied.
