Computation: comparative advantage
Result
Country A's opportunity cost of good one is 2.0000 units of good two; country B's is 1.6667. The lower opportunity cost indicates comparative advantage in good one. The model uses two countries, two goods and constant unit labour requirements; it is the Ricardian textbook case. Opportunity costs are ratios of labour requirements; absolute advantage does not determine the pattern of trade. No transport cost, tariffs, demand differences or increasing returns are included.
