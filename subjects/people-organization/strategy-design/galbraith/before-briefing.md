## Why this worksheet exists

Galbraith's Star Model bridges Lewin's change theory (force balance) and Mintzberg's structural configurations (organizational shapes). It is the middle link in the Lewin → Mintzberg chain. Without Galbraith, the jump from "change forces" to "organization shape" is too abstract.

## What you need before starting

- The same change scenario you used for Lewin (or a new one if preferred)
- Knowledge of your organization's current structure, key processes, reward systems, and people practices
- 30–45 minutes of uninterrupted time

## CHG-05 — Strategy/design chain: Galbraith entry

CHG-05 continues from CHG-04 (force balance). Before mapping the star model:

- You have identified restraining forces from Lewin.
- Now ask: which star point is misaligned with strategy and reinforcing that restraint?

**Prompt before mapping:** "Where is our organization misaligned with its own stated strategy?"

## CHG-06 — Strategy/design chain: cross-star alignment

CHG-06 follows CHG-05. Before designing an intervention:

- Structure change without process change = new org chart, same bottlenecks.
- Reward change without people change = incentives people can't act on.
- Every star point must move together.

**Prompt before intervening:** "If I change this one star point, what breaks in the other four?"

## Lewin → Galbraith handoff

From Lewin you bring: the restraining force you most want to weaken.  
Into Galbraith you ask: which star point, changed, weakens that force most directly?

Write that link down before opening `worksheet.md`.

## How to use this briefing

1. Read this document in full.
2. Write down the Lewin → Galbraith link (above).
3. Open `worksheet.md` and complete sections 1–8.
