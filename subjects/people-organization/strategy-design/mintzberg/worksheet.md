## 1. Overview

Henry Mintzberg's Five Organizational Configurations describe how organizations naturally cluster into recurring structural forms. Each configuration has a dominant coordinating mechanism, a key part of the organization, and a typical design strength/weakness:

| Configuration | Coordinating mechanism | Key part | Typical context |
|---------------|----------------------|----------|-----------------|
| Simple Structure | Direct supervision | Strategic apex | Small, young firms |
| Machine Bureaucracy | Standardization of work processes | Technostructure | Large, stable, routine |
| Professional Bureaucracy | Standardization of skills | Operating core (professionals) | Hospitals, universities, accounting |
| Divisionalized Form | Standardization of outputs | Middle management | Diversified corporations |
| Adhocracy | Mutual adjustment | Support staff / analysts | Innovation, project-based, complex |

**The core insight:** no configuration is universally best. Fit between configuration and context (size, stability, environment, technology) determines effectiveness.
