## Why this worksheet exists

Mintzberg is the end point of the Lewin → Mintzberg range. His Five Organizational Configurations answer the question "what shape should the organization take?" — which is only meaningful after you know what forces are at play (Lewin) and how design dimensions interrelate (Galbraith).

## What you need before starting

- The same change scenario from Lewin and Galbraith worksheets (for chain continuity)
- Knowledge of your organization's size, environment, technology, and decision-making patterns
- 30–45 minutes of uninterrupted time

## CHG-07 — Strategy/design chain: Mintzberg entry

CHG-07 continues from CHG-06 (cross-star alignment). Before classifying:

- You have identified a misaligned star point (Galbraith).
- Now ask: is the *whole configuration* right for this organization's context?

**Prompt before classifying:** "Is our organizational shape matched to our size, environment, and work type?"

## CHG-08 — Strategy/design chain: synthesis

CHG-08 is the chain synthesis point. Before Section 6 of the worksheet:

- Combine Lewin's restraining force + Galbraith's misalignment + Mintzberg's configuration.
- The three together explain *why* change is hard and *what* to redesign.

**Prompt before synthesizing:** "Do the three theories tell the same story about my organization?"

## CHG-13 — Strategy/design chain: cross-reference

CHG-13 ensures the Mintzberg worksheet references back to both Lewin and Galbraith worksheets. Before finishing:

- Verify you have cited at least one insight from each prior worksheet.
- The chain must be unbroken: Lewin → Galbraith → Mintzberg.

**Prompt before closing:** "Can I trace a single change problem through all three worksheets?"

## Lewin → Galbraith → Mintzberg handoff

Bring all three threads into this worksheet:

- From Lewin: your primary restraining force.
- From Galbraith: your most misaligned star point.
- Into Mintzberg: does the configuration itself create or amplify those problems?

Write these links before opening `worksheet.md`.
