## Why this worksheet exists

There were no worksheets anywhere in the Lewin → Mintzberg organizational strategy & design range. This worksheet was created to fill that gap. Lewin is the logical starting point: his Force Field Analysis and 3-Step Change Model underpin nearly every later organizational design theory.

## What you need before starting

- A real or realistic people/organization change scenario (e.g., restructure, new reporting lines, remote-work policy, merger integration)
- 30–45 minutes of uninterrupted time
- A willingness to score forces honestly (even when the scores are uncomfortable)

## CHG-03 — Strategy/design chain: entry point

This briefing opens the strategy_design chain. You are being asked to:

1. Recognize that every org design starts from a *current state* that has forces holding it in place.
2. Use Force Field Analysis to make those forces visible before designing any intervention.

**Prompt before opening the worksheet:** "What change am I trying to make, and what is keeping the organization where it is today?"

## CHG-04 — Strategy/design chain: force balance

The strategy_design chain continues from CHG-03. Before you score forces:

- Driving forces are what the organization *gains* from change.
- Restraining forces are what the organization *loses* or fears losing.
- The net force determines readiness. A positive net does not guarantee success; it only means you have permission to begin.

**Prompt before scoring:** "Which restraining force, if unaddressed, will defeat every driving force?"

## How to use this briefing

1. Read this document in full.
2. Identify your change scenario.
3. Open `worksheet.md` and complete sections 1–7.
4. Return here after completing the worksheet to reflect on the synthesis prompt (see section 5 of the worksheet).

## Escalation

If you cannot identify at least 3 driving and 3 restraining forces, your change scenario is too abstract. Pick a more concrete people/organization change and restart.
