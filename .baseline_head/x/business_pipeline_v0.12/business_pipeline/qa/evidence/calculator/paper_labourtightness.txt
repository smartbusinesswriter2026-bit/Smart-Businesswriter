Computation: labour market
Result
The real wage is 95.24 and the wage share of productivity is 0.7937. The labour market is tight at 0.2000 vacancies for each unemployed person: 10 vacancies against 50 unemployed. The real wage deflates the declared nominal wage by the declared CPI; the CPI and productivity figures are the owner's. The wage share is a simple comparison with labour productivity, not a full labour-share decomposition. No taxes, benefits, hours, composition or bargaining model is included. Tightness is the vacancies you declare divided by the unemployed you declare: a ratio of two counts, not a rate divided by anything, and not a measure of your own headcount gap.

Assumptions made
The nominal wage, CPI and productivity cover the same period and population.
The CPI is an index with a base of 100.
Productivity is output per worker.
Limitations
No hours, composition, taxes, benefits or bargaining.
The wage share is a simple ratio, not a full decomposition.
No regional or sectoral breakdown.
Tightness needs an unemployment COUNT; an unemployment rate is not an input, because a count divided by a rate has no reading.
