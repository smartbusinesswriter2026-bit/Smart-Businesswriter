Computation: fiscal multiplier
Result
With an mpc of 0.8000 and a tax rate of 0.2500, the government-spending multiplier is 2.5000, the tax multiplier is -2.0000 and the balanced-budget multiplier is 0.5000. The declared changes imply an output change of 250.00. The multiplier is a closed-economy Keynesian arithmetic identity on the declared mpc and tax rate; it is not an estimated fiscal effect. A proportional income tax is included when tax_rate is declared; a lump-sum tax change uses the same multiplier on the declared tax change. Crowding out, confidence, supply-side effects, lags and open-economy leakages are not modelled.

Assumptions made
The mpc and tax rate are constant over the relevant range.
Prices and interest rates do not respond.
There is spare capacity.
Limitations
No crowding out, confidence, supply-side or open-economy effects.
No lags or expectations.
The multipliers are arithmetic identities, not estimated policy effects.
How the figures were checked
Second implementation agrees.
Government multiplier is one over one minus mpc times one minus tax.
Tax multiplier is negative mpc times the government multiplier.
Balanced budget multiplier is one minus mpc times the government multiplier.
A higher mpc raises the government multiplier.
