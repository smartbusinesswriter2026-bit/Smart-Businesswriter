Computation: fx parity
Result
Under relative purchasing power parity the expected spot rate is 57.0980, while covered interest parity implies a forward rate of 57.0874. The declared forward is 57.0000, an annualised forward premium of 1.79%. The implied real rates are 0.96% at home and 0.98% abroad. A foreign-currency exposure of 100,000.00 hedged at 57.0000 is worth 5,700,000.00 against 5,750,000.00 unhedged, a hedge result of -50,000.00. The spot rate is quoted as domestic currency per unit of foreign currency; every other figure follows that convention. Covered interest parity and relative purchasing power parity are identities under their assumptions, not forecasts; real-world capital controls, transaction costs and risk premia are not modelled. No exchange rate, inflation rate or forward quote is retrieved or verified; all of them are declared inputs. The hedge comparison uses one declared forward and one expected spot; it is not a hedging programme, and it ignores margin, credit lines, taxes and hedge accounting.

Assumptions made
The declared rates and inflation are annual and compound over the horizon.
Capital moves freely and the two countries' assets are comparable.
The spot quote is domestic per foreign and the exposure is in foreign currency.
Limitations
Identities under assumptions, not forecasts.
No retrieved data and no transaction costs or risk premium.
One forward hedge, not an option, swap or rolling programme; no hedge accounting.
