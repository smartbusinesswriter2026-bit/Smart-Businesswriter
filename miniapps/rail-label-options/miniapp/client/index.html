1 · What the rail is actually doing

The labels are not misaligned vertically — they are too far away horizontally, and they are marked separately from their icons. These are the numbers behind that, taken from the served page (Chrome, 1280×720) and from the CSS that draws it.

Geometry of the open rail, in CSS pixels from the left edge of the strip
ElementMeasuredReading
Green strip0 → 49 pxThe icons live on green; the words live on white.
Icon glyph≈ 15 → 34 pxCentred in the strip, so 15 px of empty green sits to its right.
Label columnstarts at 67 px18 px clear of the strip (--rail-gap:18px).
Glyph → word gap33 pxThe problem. The word is not beside its icon; it is a second column.
Active rowpill at 67 → 123 pxThe mint pill wraps the word only. The active icon is not marked at all — it just gets a white glow.
Vertical≈ 1 px apartIcon centre and cap centre agree. Vertical alignment is fine.

The one-line cause. The rail is built as two columns, not one list: the icon sits on a dark green strip, the word sits on white, and 33 px of nothing sits between them. Every state that should tie the pair together — active, hover — touches only the word, so the pairing breaks exactly where you look. The three CSS levers are --rail-gap (line 29), --rail-ic-pad (line 34) and the .railtext::before highlight (lines 116–117).

Geometry cannot be fixed by the gutter alone. The distance from glyph to word is gutter + the green left to the right of the glyph. To reach a 16 px gap you have to shrink both: either the word moves left, or the icons lean toward the word. That is the fork in the road below.

What is already right

  • Vertical centring of glyph and word.
  • Every word on the same x (67 px), including “Subjects”.

What reads off

  • 33 px of dead space between every icon and its own name.
  • The active icon is marked by a glow, the active word by a pill — two different languages.
  • The hover band is painted by .railitem (lines 99–100), which spans the icon column as well, so it prints a near-white rounded band over the green behind the hovered glyph. Hover a row in the “Now” replica below to see it.

3 · Four options

Each replica below is the same markup with different geometry and different state rules. Nothing here has been applied to the site.

1

One item, one highlight

gap stays 33 px

Keep every pixel where it is; fix the states so the icon and the word are always marked together. The active icon gets a white plate on the green (its glyph flips to green-900) and the word keeps the mint pill; the hover band crosses the seam instead of stopping at it.

What changes

/* one highlight bar, crossing the seam */
.railitem::before{left:calc(var(--rail-w) - var(--rail-pad));
  right:0;top:0;bottom:0;background:var(--sunken)}
.railitem::after{left:calc(-1 * var(--rail-pad));
  width:var(--rail-w);top:0;bottom:0;
  background:rgba(255,255,255,.14)}
.railitem[aria-current=page] .ic::before{
  content:"";position:absolute;inset:0;background:#fff;
  border-radius:8px}
.railitem[aria-current=page] .ic svg{fill:var(--green-900)}
/* and the white glow on the active icon goes */

Effect and trade-off

Pairing
Explicit, not closer. The row now changes in two places at once.
Risk
Low — CSS only, ~15 lines, no geometry change.
Cost
The 33 px gap stays. The eye is told “these belong together”, but they are still far apart.
2

Lean in

gap 33 → 17.5 px

Move the word toward its icon and let the icon lean toward the word. The gutter shrinks and the icon column is right-packed inside the green instead of centred in it, so the two move toward each other from both sides.

What changes

:root{
  --rail-w:45px;      /* was 49px */
  --rail-gap:10px;   /* was 18px */
  --rail-pad:8px;    /* was 10px */
}
/* icon column right-packed instead of centred */
.railitem{padding-left:7px}   /* was var(--rail-ic-pad) */
/* --rail-text-off must move in step: 57px → 47px */

Effect and trade-off

Word lands at
55 px instead of 67 px; the glyph ends at 37.5 px.
Cost
In the collapsed rail the icons shift right ~6 px and the strip is 4 px narrower. That asymmetry is the price of pointing the icons at their words.
Risk
Medium — “Subjects” must move with the words or it desynchronises.
3

Tile

gap 33 → 14 px to the tile

Option 2's geometry, plus a hard-edged 26 px tile around every glyph. A tile gives the eye something definite to anchor on, so the word at 14 px from the tile's edge reads as the tile's caption rather than as a gap. The active row's tile fills with the subject's own colour.

What changes

/* Option 2's geometry, plus: */
.railitem .ic{
  background:rgba(255,255,255,.14);
  border-radius:8px}
.railitem[aria-current=page] .ic{
  background:var(--i)}
.railitem[aria-current=page] .ic svg{fill:#fff}

Effect and trade-off

Pairing
The strongest of the options that keep the green spine.
Cost
The strip gains a texture of tiles — more light on the green than today. The collapsed rail keeps bare glyphs, so the two states differ more than they do now.
Risk
Medium — ~20 lines and a small markup change.
4

One column, no spine

gap 33 → 12 px

Stop splitting the rail into two surfaces. The open rail loses the green spine; every subject gets a tinted 26 px tile with the glyph in its full tone, and the word starts 12 px later — the ordinary sidebar rhythm, on a single background. The green survives as the brand accent: the active wash, the focus ring, and the collapsed strip.

What changes

/* the open rail has no accent; the tile carries the colour */
body.rail-open .railaccent{display:none}
.railitem .ic{
  background:var(--tile);border-radius:8px}
.railitem{padding-left:10px;gap:12px}
/* --rail-text-x becomes 48px; --rail-text-off 38px */

Effect and trade-off

Pairing
Genuine — icon and word share one background, one rhythm, one object.
Cost
The open rail loses the green spine the current design is built around, and the collapsed→open transition has to morph.
Risk
High — ~40 lines, plus a re-look at the toggle and the strip.

4 · The type pass — applies to every option

Independently of the geometry, the word is currently the quietest thing on the row while the icon is the loudest. A 15.6 px regular word in navy-700 next to a 19 px saturated solid glyph on dark green reads as an afterthought.

.railitem .railtext{
  font-size:15px;        /* was 15.6px */
  font-weight:500;       /* was 400 */
  color:rgba(2,40,76,.88);
  letter-spacing:-.005em;}
/* leave the vertical centring alone — it is already right */
  • Active rows keep weight 600 and navy-900 — a weight step, not a colour step, so the state survives greyscale.
  • The label size change moves the cap centre by well under a pixel, so the glyph/word centring stays put.

5 · Recommendation

  1. Safe first move — Option 1 + the type pass. Nothing moves, the active row finally reads as one item, and it is about 15 lines of CSS in one file. Lowest risk, immediate improvement.
  2. If the words must actually sit with their icons — Option 3. Option 2 is the same idea in geometry only; the tile is what makes 17 px read as a pairing rather than a gap. Choose Option 2 if you would rather not add any light to the green.
  3. Option 4 only if you are willing to give up the green spine in the open state. It is the only one that makes the icon and the word the same object — but it rebuilds the rail rather than tuning it.

Whatever you pick: move --rail-text-off together with --rail-text-x. The “Subjects” heading shares the label column's x, and the last rail repair (DESIGN_NOTES §8) was exactly that heading desynchronising. Keep all six geometry variables in step and the rail stays honest.
